# The Digestive Process

The digestive system is made up of the digestive tract, also known as the gastrointestinal (GI) tract, and accessory organs.
Fun fact: because of the highly acidic conditions within the digestive tract, which could not be tolerated anywhere else in the body, the contents are considered external to the body.

## Digestive Tract

The digestive tract is the pathway of food through the body. Spanning approximately 9 meters in length, it starts at the mouth and finishes at the anus. As food travels through the tract it passes, sequentially, through these organs:

1. Mouth
2. Throat
3. Oesophagus
4. Stomach
5. Small Intestine
6. Large Intestine (Colon)
7. Rectum/Anus

## Accessory Organs

The accessory organs are named as such because food never actually enters them. Despite this they are still an essential part of the digestive process. The accessory organs are linked to the digestive tract by small tubes called `ducts` as follows:

1. Salivary Glands: connected to the mouth
2. Biliary System: connected to the Small Intestine (Liver & Gall Bladder)
3. Pancreas: connected to the Small Intestine

## What does the Digestive System do?

The role of the digestive system is to transport nutrients to the internal systems and environments of the body. It has 4 basic functions:

1. Motility: muscle contractions move food along the digestivve tract.
2. Secretions: `digestive juices` are secreted into the tract, aiding the breakdown of food.
3. Digestion: food is broken down physically, by chewing, and chemically, by digestive juices.
4. Absorption: nutrients are absorbed and transported by the blood or lymphatic system.

## Mouth

In the mouth food is physically broken down by chewing. Salivary glands also secrete the enzyme Amylase which starts the chemically digestion of Carbohydrates.
No food is absorbed in the mouth. Some medications can be absorbed in the mouth.

## Throat and Oesophagus

In the throat and oesohpagus mucus is sreted to help smooth the flow of food.
Nothing is digested or absorbed here.

## Stomach

The stomach secretes a gastric juice made up of: Hydrochloric Acid, Pepsin and Mucus. Pepsin is an enzyme that breaks down proteins. Without the mucus the Hydrochloric Acd and Pepsin would digest the stomach muscle. The stomach muscle is madeup of protein and a `gastric intrinsic factor` which is essentially for the absorption of vitamin B12.
Mainly proteins are digested in the stomach.
Compared with the Small Intestine, the stomach absorbes very little. Water and alcohol can be absorbed straight into the bloodstream, along with some medications.

## Pancreas

The Pancrease secretes enzymes, which diges carbohydrates, proteins and fats, into the Small Intestine.
As it is an accessory organ food never actually enters the Pancrease and so none is absorbed here.

## Liver

The Liver secretes Bile and Bile Salts into the Small Intestine. These aid the breakdown of fats.
Again, as the Liver is an accessory organ, food never enters it and no absorption of nutrients occurs.

## Small Intestine

Mucus, salts, and enzymes which further the digestion of carbohydrates and proteins are secreted from the lining of the Small Instestine. In the presence of the Pancreatic enzymes and Bile form the Biliary System, fat digestion completes. Carbohydrate and protein digestion continues and is completed in the lining of the Small Intestine.
All nutrients, water and most electrolytes are absorbed here. The Small Intestine is the main site of absorption in the digestive tract.

## Large Intestine (Colon)

Nothing is digested in the Large Intestine, but salt and water are absorbed. The is also absorption of some of the products formed by the bacteria within the Large Intestine, including fatty acieds and vitamins.

## Rectum/Anus

Stools ar stored in the Rectum before being expelled through the Anus.
