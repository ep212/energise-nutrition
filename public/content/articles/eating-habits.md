# Eating Habits

Have you ever considered why you eat the way you do? For most people, the only reason to eat is because you are hungry. This may be different for some, for example those looking to build muscle, but for most of us it stands true. Yet do you only ever eat when you're hungry? I know that I don't just eat when I'm hungry. For me factors like boredom and time of the day have a massive influence over what and when I eat. In this article we will explore some of the factors that affect our eating habits.

## Habit

The foods we eat and our eating behaviours are predominantly habitual. These habits can be formed at a young age, for example during childhood you may of had to eat at a certain time and you may of been told to always eat everything on your plate. Your circumstances can also influence your eating habits, if you have a busy schedule you may fall into the habit of eating fast food or regular snacking. These are just a couple of examples, but as you look to different cultures around the world you can see how many of them differ in their relationship with food. They may eat different foods, at different times, in different quantities. If you live in France you may see snails as a delicasy, whereas someone in the UK may find eating snails unimaginable. As you look at different countries and cultures it becomes clear how a persons environment can have a big impact on what and how they eat.

## Stimuli

Ivan Pavlov's experiment was able to show how certain behaviours can be learnt through association. He did this by repeatedly ringing a bell before serving a dog with food, initially the dog would only salivate in repsonse to see the food, not hearing the bell, but over time the dog began to salivate in response to hearing the bell alone. The toll of the bell had become associated with food, and the behaviour of the dog changed in response to the stimuli of the bell because of this association. We too as humans are susceptible to this, time of the day, emotions, social situations, smells, sights, sounds, tastes - to name a few - can all become things we associate with eating and can leave us salivating just like Pavlov's dog. Have you ever eaten at dinner time despite being not being hungry? Or gone for a snack because you're feeling stressed or bored? Surely the smell of your favourite meal wafting in from the kitchen has triggered some cravings! After all, this is one of the reasons supermarkets have delicious smelling bakeries sitting in store.
