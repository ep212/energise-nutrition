Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are our bodies primary source of energy. According to UK government guidelines, 50-55% of the energy we consume should come from carbohydrates. This means that 50-55% of our calorie intake should be from carbohydrates. Of this, only 5% should come from added sugars. Seen as such a large bulk of our diet should consist of carbohydrates, it's important to understand what they do, the differences between different sources, and how to make them work for you and fit your goals. Please be aware that the percentages mentioned are 'guidelines', everybody is different and people have different needs, they're not a one size fits all figure.

Types of Carbohydrates

There are two main types of carbohydrates, simple and complex. To understand the differences we first need to know that carbohydrates are made up of sugar units. Simple, or sugary, carbohydrates are made up of between 1 and 10 sugar units, where as complex, or starchy, carbohydrates can be made up of many hindreds of sugar units joined together. Monosacharrides and disaccharides are made up of one and two sugar units respectively, so they're your sugary carbohydrates. Polysaccharides are made up of many hundreds of sugar units and can be subdivided into starch and non-starch (fibre). Most of the carbohydrates we consume will be broken down into glucose during digestion, glucose is an example of a monosaccharide.
