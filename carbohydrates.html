Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are our bodies primary source of energy. According to UK government guidelines, 50-55% of the energy we consume should come from carbohydrates. This means that 50-55% of our calorie intake should be from carbohydrates. Of this, only 5% should come from added sugars. Seen as such a large bulk of our diet should consist of carbohydrates, it's important to understand what they do, the differences between different sources, and how to make them work for you and fit your goals. Please be aware that the percentages mentioned are 'guidelines', everybody is different and people have different needs, they're not a one size fits all figure.

Types of Carbohydrates

There are two main types of carbohydrates, simple and complex. To understand the differences we first need to know that carbohydrates are made up of sugar units. Simple, or sugary, carbohydrates are made up of between 1 and 10 sugar units, where as complex, or starchy, carbohydrates can be made up of many hundreds of sugar units joined together. Monosacharrides and disaccharides are made up of one and two sugar units respectively, so they're your sugary carbohydrates. Polysaccharides are made up of many hundreds of sugar units and can be subdivided into starch and non-starch (fibre). Most of the carbohydrates we consume will be broken down into glucose during digestion, glucose is an example of a monosaccharide. Starchy carbohydrates are your breads, cereals, rice, pasta and potatoes, sugary carbohydrates are sweets, chocolate, bisucites, cake, fruit and some dairy products.

How to Choose

The amount of information can be overwhelming and confusing, so here are a few guidelines to follow when trying to make healthy choices. When it comes to selecting starchy carbohydrates you want to consider managing your blood sugar levels and nutrient intake. For example, carbs in a slice of white bread are more easily absorbed than those in a sweet potato, which will lead to a spike in blood sugar levels which can fuel cravings. Also, sweet potatoes are a good source of fibre, which slows digestion, and nutrients. When it comes to sugary carbohydrates, you should aim to get these from unjuiced fruit and veg, and unsweetened dairy products. You should aim to limit processed, manufactured and sweetened foods.

What Carbohydrates Do?

Carbohydrates are your body's primary energy source, fuelling your brain, muscles and organs. They also support gut health, with fibre promoting regularity, and many whole foods are great sources of vitamins and minerals.
