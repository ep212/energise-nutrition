* Carbohydrates can generally be split into 2 types: simple and complex
* They provide a steady and constant supply of energy to the brain and CNS
* Carbs can be used immediately or stored in the muscles and liver as glycogen



**Monosaccharides, Disaccharides and Polysaccharides**

* Sugary carbs are made up of a small number of sugar units (~ 1- 10) where are starchy carbs can be made up of many hundreds
* *Monosaccharides:*
  - made up of just 1 sugar unit
  - Examples: glucose in fruit and honey, fructose in fruit and veg, galactose in milk
  - most carbs your clients eats are converted to glucose
* *Disaccharides:*
  - made up of 2 sugar units joined together
  - Examples: maltose, found in cereals such as barley, made up of 2 glucose joined together. Sucrose, more commonly known as table sugar, made up of glucose and fructose. Lactose, found in milk, made up of glucose and galactose
* *Polysaccharides:*
  - Made up of many sugar units joined together and can be subdivided into starch and non-starch (NSP)
  - Starchy polysaccharides can be further subdivided into:
  1) Resistant: amylose, resistant to digestion in the stomach and small intestine so it passes into the large intestine where it has many health benefits. Examples: cereals, corn, maize, rice, potatoes, beans, pulses, some veg.
  2) Digestible: amylopectin, easily digested in the small intestine. Found in the same foods as amylose. Different foods will have different proportions of the two
  - NSP is obtained solely of food from plant origin, and although it passes through the digestive tract largely undigested, it plays a vital roles in digestion:
  1) Soluble: helps slow digestion and absorption, in turn managing blood sugar levels. Absorbs water and forms a gel-like mass which may reduce cholesterol levels
  2) Insoluble: helps to keep the gut moving and promotes regular bowel movements. Has been linked with reducing constipation and reducing the risk of bowel cancer



**Benefits of NSP**

* Helps you to feel fuller
* Helps manage blood sugar levels and cholesterol levels
* Can lower risk of diverticular disease, cancer and diabetes
* Recommended intake is 30g per day, although currently the average intake is only 18g
